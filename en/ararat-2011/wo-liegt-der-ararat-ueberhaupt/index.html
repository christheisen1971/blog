Where is Mount Ararat, anyway?

Translated from German

Mount Ararat lies on Turkish territory and, at 5,137 m, is the highest mountain in Turkey. It is more than 300 m higher than Europe's highest mountain, Mont Blanc, and after the three Caucasus peaks Elbrus (5,642 m), Dykh-Tau (5,204 m) and Shkhara (5,202 m) it is the fourth-highest summit near Europe.

Like many other mountains, it lies in a border region – in this case in the far east, in Ağrı Province, right next to Armenia, Azerbaijan and Iran.

Historically, Ararat lay for centuries within the area settled by the Armenian people, even though "Armenia" repeatedly belonged to other empires, e.g. the Persian Empire, the empire of Alexander the Great, the Roman Empire and finally the Ottoman Empire.

Not least because of the still tense relations between predominantly Muslim Turkey, predominantly Christian Armenia and the Kurdish population, who also regard this area as part of their homeland, the region repeatedly sees armed clashes and abductions.

Ararat itself is considered sacred by Armenia's Christians, not least because of the legend that Noah's Ark is said to have come to rest on it. The whole region is in any case one of the oldest settled areas of mankind, and the original Garden of Eden is thought to have been located between the Tigris and Ararat.

Near Ararat, on a plateau at 1,719 m, lies Lake Van, which is up to 457 m deep and, at 120 km x 80 km, seven times the size of Lake Constance. The water is highly alkaline and rich in soda and other salts. You cannot drink it, but you can wash your laundry in it without soap and you can certainly swim in the lake. Lake Van belongs to Van Province, whose capital, also called Van, is the largest city in the region with around 400,000 inhabitants.
