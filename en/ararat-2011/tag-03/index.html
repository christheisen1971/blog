DEENTR

Day 3 · July 26, 2011 · 4,200 m

Acclimatisation at 4,200 m

Translated from German

Day 3 was meant for acclimatisation. We were to climb up to the second camp, but not spend the night there; instead we would go back down to base camp.

At 7 a.m. there was a simple breakfast: bread, cucumbers, tomatoes, scrambled eggs, sucuk, honey, Nutella, plus tea and coffee. The evening before, our guide Gökay had already decided that Hülya would not be allowed to climb with us. With her arm injury she wouldn't make it anyway. So the remaining 9 participants set off at 8 o'clock sharp.

At first we walked through green terrain for a while. Even the first steps were hard: the thin air, the short, uncomfortable night and the tiredness in our bones made themselves felt. Gönül gave out after a short while and gave up. She walked back alone to base camp to join Hülya.

The route soon became very rocky and steeper. Every step had to be chosen carefully. Our eyes were always directed forwards and down. At first it was still cool, but soon it got quite warm. I walked in three-quarter-length trousers and a T-shirt. When the wind picked up, I put on my fleece jacket.

As on the two previous days, our packed lunch consisted only of chocolate bars, fruit and 1.5 litres of water. We had also brought chocolate, dried fruit and nuts ourselves. Harald, Ahmet and I didn't think it was enough for the tour. The sweet stuff lay heavy in Ahmet's stomach, so he did without it altogether and preferred nuts and dried fruit. The daily food was really one of the biggest minuses of the tour.

The landscape wasn't very varied. The rock was mostly black. Along the way we were able to admire some very interesting black, flat stones, and Ahmet enthused that you could make a great "hot stone" out of them. So he resolved to take a nice specimen home with him on the way back. And anyone who knows Ahmet knows that he means something like that seriously.

At about 4,000 metres we reached the first snowfields. From there a very long, steeply sloping snowfield ran up to our destination, Camp 2 at 4,163 m, which we reached after 3:13 hours of climbing. There we rested first. Harald had raced ahead of the group with the second guide and had already been sunbathing for a good half hour. When the rest of the group arrived exhausted, everyone looked for a more or less smooth rock and rested. During the ascent we had seen and overtaken several groups. Besides us there was a group from Iran and one from Russia on the mountain. The Russians had had a good drink the evening before and were struggling up, exhausted.

After about an hour's rest we started the descent. Anyone who thinks the descent is easier is mistaken. You have to pay very close attention with every step. There is scree everywhere, and seemingly solid rocks turn out to be loose. Without a trekking pole it is very dangerous. Of course the descent is quicker, and so we were back at base camp at about 2:30 p.m. Once there, we immediately took off our boots and gave our feet a cooling bath in the ice-cold stream.

There were pastries, tea and coffee. Some rested, others chatted or prepared their daypacks for the next day. At sunset we had dinner together again. Then we all fell into bed exhausted. The next day we wanted to get up as early as possible so as not to "get stuck in traffic" and to get the best possible tent spot up at Camp 2.
