Day 1 · July 24, 2011

Off we go

Translated from German

Today we set off. Harald had stayed the night at our place, and Ahmet picked us up by car in Beylerbeyi at 5:45 a.m. Gülay, Ahmet's wife, was kind enough to drive us to the airport. The two of them had barely slept an hour, because there had been a dangerous fire in the neighbourhood during the night.

At that time of day the drive to the airport took less than 20 minutes, and check-in and boarding were done quickly. The Turkish Airlines flight took about 2 hours, and only then do you notice that Istanbul lies pretty much exactly halfway between our destination, Van, and Harald's departure point, Vienna.

As expected, Van Airport was very basic. The building is small and old. A new terminal is being built next door. After landing we had to wait for the other 7 participants. Mehmet, Adam, Gönül, Fetiye and Hülya were on the next plane, and after about an hour a minibus took us into town for breakfast. The last participants, Salim and Meral, came on a later flight from Ankara. We had breakfast in an apparently notorious breakfast restaurant. It was full of Turkish and foreign mountaineering tourists. The owner was a frustrated entertainer: he ordered his waiters around like a madman while regaling the guests with anecdotes and poems. Every few minutes he handed out little souvenirs and scarves to one guest or another. A very interesting experience.

After breakfast we continued by bus towards Doğubeyazıt.

First we drove past the impressive Lake Van. It is seven times the size of Lake Constance and almost a small sea. After about an hour we took a break at the impressive Muradiye waterfall. There was tea and we busily took photos. Unfortunately it also started to rain, so we quickly got back on the road.

We continued onto the plateau along the Turkish-Iranian border and through fairly barren country. It was marked by scattered livestock farming and grain fields. There were hardly any bushes or trees. Everywhere there were whole fields of black volcanic rock. At one point we reached an altitude of 2,600 m. The weather was cool and rainy. Shortly before our destination the road went back down to about 1,500 m to Doğubeyazıt. From there we had our first view of Ararat. It towers majestically over the landscape next to Küçük Ağrı, which is more than 1,000 m lower.

Doğubeyazıt is – like all the other towns in the region – very drab and simple. The population is predominantly Kurdish, but everything is written in Turkish. In town we stocked up one last time on chocolate, biscuits, muesli bars and walnuts. Then we went to the Hotel SIM-ER. It is very simple, but OK.

Harald, Ahmet and I shared a room for three. There we repacked our bags and prepared our daypacks and clothes for the next day. Afterwards we walked once more to the main road – Harald barefoot, of course – and took a few photos and a video of Ararat.

Unfortunately clouds moved in and it started to rain, so we had to go back.

We had dinner in the hotel dining room. For my taste there were too many flies buzzing around. But it was important to take in enough carbohydrates. To finish, we had tea on the hotel terrace and then went to bed relatively early. We were all tired, and we had an early start the next morning
