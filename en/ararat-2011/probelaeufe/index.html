DEENTR

June 17, 2011

Test hikes

Translated from German

While I had only prepared for the Himalaya trek with running, this time I also put a mountain ascent into the warm-up programme. To be precise: there were two ascents, which I did together with my friend Ahmet, who will also be taking part in the Ararat tour.
We chose Kartepe in Kocaeli, about 100 km east of Istanbul, which is a respectable 1,699 m high.

The first ascent was on 28 May 2011. However, apart from an asphalt road that is not much fun to walk on, Kartepe is only accessible via forestry tracks. And on the first ascent we didn't find them at all at first. So the first attempt was more of a cross-country run through dense jungle. Luckily we (Ahmet, a friend and I) had a machete with us. The weather was very foggy and cold, too. In the end the route was 9.22 km long, which we finished in 3 hours and 50 minutes. We started up the mountain at 170 m near the village of Balaban and reached an altitude of 1,220 m. Because of the exhausting route and the bad weather, we stopped at the asphalt road and had ourselves picked up. We skipped the way up to the ski area and the Green Park Resort there. I created the profile shown here with the iPhone app Endomondo. The route was still very beautiful. At first we walked along a stream and even found 2 tortoises. Later we came across a wide forestry track, which made walking easier. Only in between was it very adventurous.

So for the next attempt I decided to scout out a proper hiking route. But that is easier said than done, because as far as I know there are no hiking maps for Kartepe. So I searched for possible paths using the satellite view in Google Maps. I found one from the village of Suadiye to the Green Park Resort. I then marked the waypoints online with GPSies.com and got a pretty good route. Anyone interested can find it via this link. But now I faced another problem: how do you get the map onto the iPhone? There are various apps that can do this, but they need internet access to display the maps. There are also apps for which you can download maps, but there is no map of Kartepe. Then I found the solution: with the app Gaia GPS you can first view and save the available maps online, and the app then combines them with the route imported from GPSies. So you can go through the terrain offline – but you mustn't get too lost, because without a 3G signal there are no online maps.

Ahmet and I (plus another friend) started the second attempt on 17 June 2011. The route was 10.12 km long with a total ascent of 1,202 m. It took us (including breaks) 4 hours and 18 minutes. The average speed was 2.3 km/h.

The vegetation is very beautiful. Everything is green. The forest is very dense. In places the ground is very loamy and reddish-brown. Along the way we found lots of little wild strawberries, which we feasted on. This time too it was hazy and humid and later foggy. At one point we got caught in a short rain shower, which caught Ahmet in particular on the wrong foot, because he hadn't brought a rain jacket. So we learned something again: the weather in the mountains is very unpredictable and can turn from one minute to the next. You always have to be well equipped – even on such a small mountain. My preparation with the map also proved very worthwhile. We often came to forks in the path where, without the iPhone, we really wouldn't have known which way to go. And you can quickly get lost in an area like this. Making progress isn't that trivial, and to be honest I wouldn't want to stumble through the forest in the dark and run into bears.

For me, the two test runs were definitely worthwhile. First, I broke in my boots again. Where they pinch, some calluses have already formed. In my opinion, the difficulty of the stage is already pretty close to what awaits us on Ararat. There, too, we will climb about 1,000 metres of altitude per day, with 4–5 hours of walking. And I also enjoyed the nature and the solitude. That really does you good when you live all the time in a mega-city of 15 million people.
