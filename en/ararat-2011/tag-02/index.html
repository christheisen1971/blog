DEENTR

Day 2 · July 25, 2011 · 3,346 m

Setting off for base camp

Translated from German

The night in the hotel was OK under the circumstances. Just too short, of course. Breakfast was at 6:30 a.m., a typical breakfast for a one- or two-star Turkish hotel: black tea or instant coffee, cucumbers, tomatoes, white bread, melon, hard-boiled eggs and pre-packed mini portions of butter, honey and jam.

After breakfast we got a kind of packed lunch consisting of chocolate bars, a banana and 1.5 litres of water. I poured the water into the hydration bladder in my daypack. We set off in a minibus at 7 a.m. Shortly after the hotel we left the main road and drove towards the mountain on a rather rough gravel track. Our luggage was stowed on the roof of the bus, our daypacks on our laps. The drive was arduous and long. At last we came to the village of Eliköy, which consists of a few very simple stone houses. We could also make out a village school, but it looked very deserted, and it wasn't clear whether that was just because of the school holidays.

Just past Eliköy we got out at an altitude of 2,169 m, and the luggage was transferred to horses that were already waiting for us. The horses were to carry our luggage, the tents, kitchen equipment and food up to base camp independently of us. For us it was now time, at 7:48 a.m., to continue on foot. At first we followed the gravel track, but soon we were crossing meadows.

Since Ararat is a volcano, there are rocks and boulders of all sizes lying around everywhere. The higher you go, the more frequent and larger the rocks become. So every step has to be chosen carefully. Shortly after we started climbing, it was clear that Hülya – at 59 the oldest participant, and also handicapped by an arm injury – would not keep up with the group's pace. She fell behind, and in the end our guide continued climbing alone with her at a slow pace. The rest of the group went on ahead with the second guide, a Kurd.

Halfway up we passed a kind of yurt, an open tent in which a Kurdish family lives. A sign announced it as the "Ararat Café". The head of the family, surely a shepherd too, offered the usual cold drinks, black tea or knitwear presumably made by his wife. I gave the shepherd's two daughters, Sevda and Yıldız, a few chocolate bars from my packed lunch. It was very hot. Ahmet, Harald and I had a tea. Then we soon moved on.

The surroundings were still very green. But here too there were neither trees nor bushes. Interestingly, there was quite a lot of livestock, even though the plateau further down would be much more accessible for grazing and apparently isn't used.

The climb on the first day was really quite strenuous. We tried to find a rhythm, but with all the rocks it wasn't easy. Finally, at exactly 3,200 metres, after exactly 4.5 hours and 6 km, we reached the first camp. But we celebrated too soon: this part of the camp was already full. So we had to climb about 150 metres higher, which took us another hour. At 1:20 p.m. we reached our destination: base camp.

We were to spend three nights at this base camp. In the meantime, the tents and the kitchen tent had already been put up by the cook, the cook's assistant and the second guide who accompanied us. Harald and I took a two-man tent, Ahmet a two-man tent all to himself. A sleeping mat was laid in each tent as an underlay, and we put our own mats on top. It had been hot all day. Even now it was still very warm. Two other groups were camping there, and unfortunately they played loud music and danced. A small stream ran through the camp where you could wash. There was no toilet. Instead, when the time came, everyone looked for a nice big rock in the surroundings.

The cook prepared a dinner, which we ate together at a long, low, improvised table. To drink there was water, tea and instant coffee.

As soon as the sun disappeared behind the mountain, it got noticeably cold. You couldn't sit outside any more without a fleece jacket. So everyone disappeared into their tents fairly early. Since the ground wasn't level, I – and apparently Harald too, as he told me the next day – kept sliding sideways in my sleeping bag. It wasn't very cold in the tent. I slept with my sleeping bag half open.
