September 10, 2011

Looking back

Translated from German

In hindsight, things always become a little rose-tinted. That is especially true, in the case of my mountain tours, of the hardships while walking. I have to admit that more than once during the ascent or descent I asked myself why on earth I had come up with this stupid idea. But as soon as you reach the summit, it is all forgotten. You only remember the good hours and the beautiful views. And maybe you are already planning the next adventure. In concrete terms, that means the bike tour from Berlin to Istanbul next year – but more about that in another blog.

At this point, a few positive and negative things about the tour and perhaps a few tips:

I was positively surprised by the whole region of Eastern Anatolia. Turkey truly has a treasure here, and I hope that it will develop tourism carefully but steadily. I liked it very much there.

The security situation was also fine. At no point did I feel unsafe in any way, even though the conflict between the military and terrorists had escalated again only shortly before. There was no military or increased police presence on the streets either. So you shouldn't always let the press drive you crazy, because a lot is blown up for political reasons. We, at any rate, relied on the tour operator. I think he would have cancelled the tour if there had been real security concerns.

Only on this trip did I become aware of how close Iran is. When we lived in Dubai, Iran lay across the Gulf. Now at Ararat – after all, more than 2 hours' flight from Dubai – we were at the Iranian border again. And the massive mountains on the Iranian side were new to me as well. It is certainly a very interesting country, and I hope that one day it will be easier to visit it.

On the negative side were some aspects of the organisation. Of course everything is still quite basic, especially on the official side. There are no permanent huts, no proper water supply or waste water disposal, and rubbish is a huge problem. Not to mention the non-existent toilets, which didn't bother us, though. As we heard from our companions on the trip, a lot is being planned, and I expect that many things will improve soon.

As for the organisation of the tour itself, I would like to criticise the food. Firstly, the amount of water was not sufficient. Secondly, at midday we got packed lunches consisting only of chocolate, biscuits and one piece of fruit. That is decidedly too little and too one-sided. Nuts and dried fruit, for example, are much better sources of energy and are also plentiful in the region. So my tip: on arrival, ask the guide what the food will be like and then perhaps buy a few things at the last stop before the hotel. We, at any rate, had too much chocolate with us.

Finally, a few tips:

  1. Good equipment. There's no way around it: good boots. Thick socks (even when it's warm). At least one trekking pole. Hat and gloves. Rain trousers and rain jacket. Down jacket. Sleeping bag and sleeping mat. Suitable underwear – i.e. nothing made of cotton. A quick-dry towel that doesn't soak up moisture. Headlamp. A good hiking backpack. Blister plasters and tape. Sunscreen. Sunglasses. Possibly earplugs, because you sleep several to a tent. And please don't take a suitcase into the mountains!
  2. Be sure to break in your boots well in advance, so that the skin can toughen up at the friction points. Ahmet and I had climbed about 1,000 metres twice beforehand. But we hadn't walked back down, so it was on the descent that I got blisters on my big toes (on the outside). Because when you descend, your toes slide forward. You have to use blister plasters early and perhaps fix them with tape. And then don't take them off again, just keep walking – that, at least, is my experience after Nepal and Ararat.
  3. Respect the mountain and nature. Stay with the group. Always drink enough. Never deliberately kick stones downhill – they can trigger an avalanche and injure others (which nearly happened with us because of a stupid mistake of mine).

Perhaps this blog, or the one about my trek to Mount Everest Base Camp, has made one or two readers want to go on a similar adventure. That would make me happy. If you have any questions, feel free to e-mail me at christian(a)theisen.net.
