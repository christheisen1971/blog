Day 4 · July 27, 2011 · 4,117 m

Climbing to Camp 2

Translated from German

The night was once again rather mixed. In the mornings it was always very cold until the sun's rays could warm us up. As soon as they reached us, though, it got warm quite quickly.

That morning everything had to happen very fast: get up, get dressed, pack your bag, have breakfast, take down the tent. Since Hülya and Gönül couldn't climb any further, our guide Gökay had decided to leave the kitchen tent, a tent for the two women and the cook's assistant at base camp. Everything else was loaded onto the horses while we were still having breakfast. The horses belong to Kurdish horsemen who do nothing but ride up and down the mountain with their horses. The horses are often accompanied by teenagers and children. Apparently the group leaders "book" the horses in advance, so that they are at the tents in time for loading. In any case, we met the same horses and horsemen at different altitudes on the mountain. Even young foals have to go up and down the mountain with their mothers and so get used to these hardships.

We already knew the climb to Camp 2 from the day before. Since I didn't know whether the iPhone battery would last for GPS tracking with Endomondo, I had already recorded the ascent the day before. So I didn't record anything on this day.

I assume that we again needed about 3 hours for the roughly 800 metres of altitude. In any case, we camped about 50 metres lower than the point we had reached the day before. Up there it had apparently already been full. I would have preferred to climb higher so as not to have to do this stretch again the next day. Harald had of course raced ahead again that day and had stopped somewhere further up. Since we didn't come up to him, after waiting a long time he came back down to us.

In the meantime Ahmet and I had picked a spot for our tent and levelled the ground with German thoroughness. That night the three of us were to sleep in one tent. Firstly, because there were only a few tent spots up here anyway, and secondly, because it would be too cold in the tent with just two. When the horses finally arrived and we fetched a tent and wanted to put it up, we realised that the tent was too small for the pitch, which I had neatly bordered with dry-stone walls. With heavy hearts we had to tear down all the walls, and only just managed to fit the tent onto the intended spot.

We had arrived at Camp 2 relatively early. Soon after putting up the tent there was to be "dinner". The sun would set early, and it would be too cold to sit outside together anyway. So we prepared our things for the next day and gathered around Gökay, who wanted to teach us how to climb with crampons and an ice axe.

We still hoped that the snowfields would still be hard enough on the ascent if we set off early enough. But since that couldn't be guaranteed, each of us was given suitable crampons and an ice axe. On a nearby snowfield, Gökay showed us how to put on the crampons, how to climb with them, how to use the ice axe and what you must never do. Everyone then took their crampons and ice axe and strapped them to their backpack.

Then it was already time for dinner. At around 4:30 p.m. we retreated into our tents, because we wanted to set off at 2 a.m. at the latest. Our supposed three-man tent was more of a two-and-a-half-man tent, and the three of us lay in it like sardines. Ahmet and Harald lay on the outside with their heads towards the tent entrance, me in the middle with my head at their feet. Even though we were pretty worn out from the day, it was of course hard to fall asleep straight away. It was early, cramped, stuffy and warm. With the sleeping bag open, it quickly got chilly. Every movement could disturb the others, so you hardly dared to move. All in all, I can only stress once again: camping really isn't for me.
