Stage 1 · Hiking day 2 · 28 September 2025

High above Ölüdeniz and lunch in Karaağaç

Translated from German

The second day began with a view from the hotel room of the mountains above Ölüdeniz.

This was the start: the gate of the Likya Yolu, as the Lycian Way is called in Turkish, above Ölüdeniz. A signboard shows the whole route as far as Antalya.

A little later we reached a viewpoint. Far below us lay the bay and the lagoon of Ölüdeniz, with paragliders floating in the sky.

The path continued high above the coast, again and again with views down to the beach of Ölüdeniz. On a rock a ginger cat crossed our path – with the bay in the background probably the most photogenic encounter of the day.

During the morning we took a few more souvenir photos along the way.

For lunch we stopped at the Çeşme Başı Café in the village of Karaağaç. In the courtyard under a grapevine there was rice, vegetable dishes, salad, yoghurt, ayran and tea.

After the meal we relaxed over a glass of tea, feet up, looking out into the green and at the mountains in the clouds.

Above us hung the ripe grapes of the arbour, and in the garden a pomegranate tree was bearing fruit.

Chickens roamed freely in the village, and on a flat roof finely chopped red peppers were drying in the sun – probably for pul biber, the pepper flakes found on almost every table in Turkey.

Shortly after lunch we met a cow that had made herself comfortable in the middle of the gravel track.

Then the last stretch of the day began. By the path stood a donkey that was happy to be stroked – Nesli, our guide and the donkey's owner were soon chatting away with it.

Green cones hung on the pines. The most common tree on this coast is the Turkish red pine or Calabrian pine (Pinus brutia, kızılçam in Turkish). Its cones sit almost without a stalk directly on the twig and take two years to ripen. The trees cope well with heat and drought and cover large parts of the Lycian mountains; they are also behind the pine honey (çam balı) the Muğla region is known for.

The path climbed once more, and far below us lay the Ölüdeniz lagoon one last time. Nesli sat down on a rock above the drop for a photo.

During a break Nesli took a selfie with the whole group.

In the late afternoon clouds drifted over the mountains. We went downhill on a wide gravel track between olive trees.

The day's destination was Faralya. The watch showed 10.37 kilometres and 532 metres of ascent; including all breaks we were on the move for a little over five hours.
