DEENTR

Stage 1 · Hiking day 4 · 30 September 2025

From the old tree to the Yediburunlar lighthouse

Translated from German

The last hiking day began with lovely weather. At the hotel two cats had already taken possession of my backpack, and the pool lay perfectly still in the morning light.

From Kabak, where the hike had ended the day before, we took the bus up into the mountains. On the way up, wisps of fog drifted out of the valleys. By the roadside stood an old trailer and a rusty water tank painted with the crescent and star.

On the way the bus stopped at an old tree on the mountain road above Alınca, below the Alınca View Point and near Astipalya Glamping, at about 1,060 metres the highest point of our whole trip – reached by bus, though, not on foot. We took a group photo, and a few minutes later I made friends with a goat.

The tree is a cedar of Lebanon (Cedrus libani, Toros sediri in Turkish). It grows in the Taurus Mountains, mostly between about 1,000 and 2,000 metres; the largest cedar forests in the world are in Turkey today. Typical are the wide, horizontal branches in tiers and the cones sitting upright on the twigs, which you can make out at the top of the tree in the selfie.

Afterwards we drove down to Alınca, and that is where we set off. The start was on Alınca Sokak in the mountain village of Alınca, by the Likya Restaurant Kamp, at a good 600 metres. The path first passed Haus Alınca to the viewpoint above the bay of Balartı (Cennet Bay), then wound down in switchbacks and ran south high above the coast. On the last kilometres it climbed again along Yediburunlar Sokak. The finish was a point just short of the Yediburunlar lighthouse (“Seven Capes”). All day the altitude stayed between about 430 and 660 metres.

The path now ran high above the cliffs. At a rocky edge a green arrow pointed the way, with bays and headlands far below us in the deep blue sea.

Gnarled conifers grew between the rocks, their roots clawing into the scree – cedars and junipers that defy wind and drought up here.

Shortly before midday I took a break on a boulder, with a turquoise bay far below me.

Shortly afterwards we stood in front of a round building with a low stone dome: an old cistern. Through an opening in the wall you could look inside – under the masonry vault there was still clear water. Domed rainwater stores like this can be found all along the Lycian Way. There are hardly any springs in this dry karst landscape, so the winter rain was collected and stored for the long summer. Many date back to ancient or Byzantine times and are still used by shepherds today.

On the last stretch Deniz and Lütfü also took a break on a boulder.

At the end the watch showed 6.09 kilometres and 336 metres of ascent.

Afterwards we sat together for a long time on the veranda of a village house. Old tools and blue nazar amulets against the evil eye hung on the wall, and a big pot of basil stood on the table.

After the last day's stage the bus dropped us at the Dalaman Airport Alibaba Hotel, where we spent the last night. The last photo of the tour shows the group in front of the bus.
