DEENTR

Day 11 · September 28, 2009

Over the Cho La Pass (5,370 m) to Lobuche (4,900 m)

Translated from German

If I wrote that yesterday was hell, I didn't yet know what today would bring.

Unfortunately the day was ill-starred. I had felt sick all night and so had hardly slept. The alarm went off at 4:30. Apart from brushing my teeth under the starry Nepali sky, I skipped the quick wash entirely today. Breakfast was at 5:00, so I had to pack the duffel bag beforehand. I didn't eat any breakfast at all because I still felt too sick. Instead I bought 2 Snickers and a bottle of Coke. Sugar is always good.

The climb began quite soon after leaving the lodge. For about 3 km we had to manage a still relatively gentle incline. Even so, it's torture at this hour. Then we reached a rise from which we could see the pass we were to cross today (see photo: the pass is in the middle between the two peaks). It's incredibly steep and high. To get there we had to descend once more and cross a valley full of boulders. That stretch alone was inhuman.

And then the climb to the pass began. We had to overcome about 600 metres of ascent. Rubble, stones, boulders. All covered in snow. A path was barely recognisable. To the side it dropped away steeply. One wrong step and it would have been over. And I have to admit that at this point my strength gave out. Every 2–3 steps I had to stop to bring my pulse down. Strangely, every few minutes, out of the blue, I had to take 5–6 very quick breaths. I simply hadn't eaten anything, and I just wasn't well that day.

Still, I made it – very slowly and with the help of the other guys. And then, at the top at 5,370 m, an incredibly beautiful sight opened up. Everything white with snow. A huge glacier that we got to walk along. Through the snow. The other mountains formed an incredible backdrop. The snow was so bright that you couldn't stand it without sunglasses. Unique. The torments of the morning – it had taken us 5 hours to get there – were forgotten. But let me say once more that this stage was incredibly hard and, in my opinion, even dangerous.

At the end of the glacier we could already see our actual destination for the day in the distance, Dzongla (4,650 m). But it took us more than another hour to descend there. The descent was as difficult as it was dangerous. To paint a picture: the regions we hiked through look exactly like those in the "Lord of the Rings" trilogy.

There are stones and rocks so beautiful you want to take them all with you. Many stones sparkle in a very peculiar way. Then there are many stones whose red betrays iron or whose green betrays copper. Because you have to watch the path almost all the time, you see so many stones. It's impossible to describe in words.

When we finally arrived in Dzongla after 7 hours, our worst fears came true. The lodge is pretty much the last hole you'd want to stay in. There's one room with wooden platforms on which several mattresses lie side by side (see photo). So you sleep side by side with strange trekkers. Our decision to move straight on to Lobuche after lunch was made immediately. So we ate something quickly and set off again right away.

The path had about 250 m of ascent and was actually quite OK. We had planned another 3 hours, and that's what it took. So in total we hiked a net 10 hours. And the pass is probably the hardest stage of the whole tour. In any case we even needed our rain gear on the last stretch, because it started to snow. So I did get to use those clothes after all. At least.

The lodge here in Lobuche is quite OK. The rooms are basic and cold, but everything seems clean. I even phoned Yesim and Dicle, because there's a landline. Our mobile phones still have no reception.

Given that we're all completely exhausted and haven't showered for days, we'll stay here an extra day. I just want to sleep.

Lobuche – like the other stops – isn't a village as such. It's a cluster of several guesthouses. Nothing more.

So tomorrow I'll sleep, shower, do laundry and simply be bored. How lovely.
