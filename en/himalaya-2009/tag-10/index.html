DEENTR

Day 10 · September 27, 2009

Gokyo Ri & Dragnag

Translated from German

Today was marked by 2 real highlights: first, climbing our first 5,000-metre peak (Gokyo Ri), and second, crossing a glacier.

The first part, the ascent of Gokyo Ri, was hell. I set my alarm for 3:30 and we met in the lounge at 3:50. There was a little tea, and I ate some biscuits and an energy bar. We set off without a proper breakfast. Gokyo Ri is 5,350 m high, and from our lodge it was about 600 m of ascent to the top.

With the guide we were 6 people and wore head torches (see photo with Harald), because it was pitch dark outside. And bitterly cold. First we had to cross a shallow river that flows into the lake. So we jumped from stone to stone over the water in the beam of our head torches. What a start to the tour.

The whole ascent took about 2.5 hours – sheer torture. It's indescribable. Alexander went ahead once again; we lost sight of him in the dark fairly quickly. The rest of the group stayed together, even though Harald at least could have gone faster.

When we reached the top, the fabulous view of course made up for the hardship. The sun had already risen. The view of the surrounding mountains and especially of the glacier (see photo) was impressive. We took lots of photos up there and then went back down. The descent was easier, of course, but still very strenuous.

At the lodge we met Ralf, had breakfast, packed our things and said goodbye to Stefan and Sandra. They booked a shorter tour and are now on their way back to Kathmandu.

Ralf, Harald, Alexander and I set off shortly afterwards for Dragnag, a place just before the pass towards Mount Everest that we'll cross the following day.

The path first led from Gokyo up the lateral moraine (see photo with lake) and then right across a huge glacier. I had never seen anything like it up close, let alone walked on a glacier. It's unimaginable. All you see is a huge pile of stones and rubble. You'd think it was a quarry. In between there are occasional lakes in the glacier. There you can see how thick the glacier is, because the sides dropping down to the water are pure ice. The stones and rocks are incredibly beautiful. At first (and distant) glance everything is grey on grey, but once you're down there every stone looks different. Many are marbled or sparkle brightly. I'd have loved to take lots of them with me, but unfortunately that wasn't possible. Dicle would have been thrilled.

I walked the route in trainers because I have problems with the Achilles tendon in my right foot. After about 1.5 hours we reached the lodge, which makes a good impression.

We spent the rest of the day in the common room, had lunch and dinner and did some laundry. Later it even snowed, so we don't know whether the pass crossing will work out tomorrow. In any case we have to get up early again tomorrow – we're leaving at 5:30. The pass is long and we won't be able to stop for about 7–8 hours. So good night for now.
