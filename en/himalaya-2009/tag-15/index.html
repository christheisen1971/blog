DEENTR

Day 15 · October 2, 2009

Tengboche – 3,860 m

Translated from German

Our last stage took us to Tengboche. The last stage for Ralf and me, anyway. Alexander and Harald will make a detour and then finish the tour. Ralf and I have decided to fly back to Kathmandu by helicopter from Tengboche tomorrow, because we already know the next two stages (Namche Bazaar and Lukla) and want to fly back as soon as possible. Thanks to the double stages and crossing the Cho La Pass we gained 3 days in total, and of course we can't force the others to end the tour early. Alexander and Harald would like to stay in the Khumbu region for the full time. And rightly so.

Anyway, today's stage was very beautiful. It went largely downhill. The vegetation changed quickly, and we once again walked through what you could call villages. The path led us along green mountainsides, with a wild river flowing in the valley below. Sometimes it went uphill, but mostly downhill. That can still be difficult, though, and it certainly takes its toll on ligaments and knees. I especially feel sorry for Ralf with his bursitis – I wonder how he managed all this anyway.

Our lodge is about 200 m below Tengboche – actually in Deboche. Comparative luxury. Soft cots, and the climate isn't so icy any more. The toilets are so-so, and the shower was apparently good, I'm told.

After checking in and having lunch we went up to the Buddhist monastery in Tengboche at 2:30 pm. Next to it is the heliport, from where we'll fly tomorrow. Well, you can hardly call it a heliport. It's a square of about 25 square metres paved with rough stones. I'm curious about tomorrow.

At 3 pm the monks blew on conch shells to signal that the ceremony was beginning. We went – without shoes – into the prayer hall of the monastery and sat down on the floor at the side. There's a public prayer session here every day at 3 pm.

6–7 monks came, 4 of them little boys, sat cross-legged on benches inside and pulled on their burgundy robes. Then they began to hum, mumble and recite prayers quickly and melodically. Somehow not in unison, but in a way that created a melody. Very interesting, but also quite sobering when you listen to it for 30 minutes. In between, a little monk poured hot tea into his fellow monks' bowls. Every 10 minutes there was a 2–3-minute tea break. Then the prayers continued. We left after about 30 minutes.

We didn't visit any other monasteries, though according to the map there are some in the area. We all went back to the lodge and got ready for dinner. We played cards and went to bed early as always.
