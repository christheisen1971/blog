Day 16 · October 3, 2009

Kathmandu

Translated from German

The day Ralf and I had been looking forward to: the flight back to Kathmandu – by helicopter. Admittedly we were a little uneasy. The technology here in Nepal is surely not always flawless. And we had already got to know the winds ourselves. Oh well.

The helicopter was due to land up by the monastery at 9:00. So we had to get up early, have breakfast and climb the 200 m from the lodge up to Tengboche again. By the way, that was quite a steep climb – I didn't mention that yesterday. Ralf had given his boots to our guide Ram, and I had packed mine in my luggage. So we walked the route in trainers.

We were up there at 9:00 – but the weather was very overcast. So it was more than doubtful whether the helicopter could come at all. We sat down in a lodge, drank tea and played cards. Alexander and Harald were there too, as they had to walk that way anyway and then continue from there to Namche Bazaar.

Ram, our guide, phoned the helicopter and found out that it hadn't even taken off in Kathmandu (about an hour away), because it was pouring with rain there. Basically today was the first time we had such bad luck with the weather.

After a long wait we got word that the helicopter had arrived at Lukla airport and was being refuelled. After more waiting we were told it had taken off. So another 20 minutes or so.

In the meantime we had said goodbye to Alexander and Harald, as they still had a long stage ahead of them and it wasn't certain when the helicopter would arrive.

The helicopter then landed in a meadow rather than on the heliport. Before that, a local had asked us whether he could fly with us because his sister had fallen ill in Kathmandu. That was surely a barefaced lie, but we took the man along. Maybe it'll bring us luck one day.

The flight was beyond crazy. The helicopter only stayed on the ground in Tengboche for a few minutes, because the clouds kept closing in. It wanted to get out of that pea soup as quickly as possible. First we flew for about 10 minutes to Lukla. We landed there briefly, stowed the luggage better and refuelled again. Then we continued. The helicopter had to fly quite high because it kept having to cross one ridge or another. And behind each ridge it dropped away steeply, so the updrafts rocked the helicopter hard. Sometimes that wasn't funny at all.

The flight took about 50 minutes. Words can't describe it. Incredible. I took photos and Ralf filmed. Below us it got greener and greener, and you could clearly see the terraced fields. And finally we were in Kathmandu. At the airport. Landed safely. Phew!

From the airport we took a taxi to the Hyatt Hotel (see photo: lobby), the best address in town. We had booked 2 rooms there beforehand. Luckily they even upgraded us to 2 suites. And of course they were a very stark contrast to what we had experienced over the last two weeks: soft beds, a great shower and all the trimmings. I don't need to describe it in detail. You can imagine.

In any case, the first thing we did was eat well. I started with a big salad. Fresh salad. Yesss. And later fruit too. Man, how I had missed salad and fruit. Always just noodles, rice and potatoes. Nobody can stand that. OK, between salad and fruit there were also a few trips to the main part of the buffet, steaks & co. Mmmmm…

We simply enjoyed the day. By the way, there were 2 power cuts – once I was just in the shower with shampoo in my hair. Well, luckily nothing happened. The hotel surely has a comprehensive plan B for such cases. Because in Kathmandu the power grid sometimes goes down for 18 hours.

Finally we fell into bed dead tired. And what beds :)
