DEENTR

Day 2 · September 19, 2009

Kathmandu

Translated from German

Today was a long day. Actually everything went the way we'd imagined. The flights were very early, but pleasant.

We landed in Kathmandu at around 6 pm local time. The visa formalities were done quite quickly. You pay about 40 EUR per person. In front of the visa counter I quickly changed some euros into rupees (the rate is about 1:10, by the way), but nobody wanted their own currency for the visa fee.

The chaos hit us at baggage claim, though. There was really only one baggage belt, and everybody was crowding around it. People took suitcases off the belt that weren't theirs and then left them lying somewhere. About an hour after landing we were finally out of the airport, which really consists of just one terminal.

The tour operator met us and about 20 other participants at the exit. Then the luggage was loaded into minibuses, and so were we – but not before being cheekily asked for a big tip, several times. I gave 10 EUR for the two of us – which is quite a lot – and no sooner had I handed it over than the demand came: "Another 10!" I've never experienced anything like it. Of course I didn't give any more – they must be joking.

The drive to the hotel was an adventure. It was dark and very chaotic. They drive on the left here. The city is very crowded, there are shops and food stalls everywhere, and the roads are very bad. Apart from the rather pushy people at the airport, the rest of the Nepalis seem peaceful and friendly.

Our hotel was quite decent and had 3 (Nepali) stars. Our room was very basic but even had a TV, air conditioning and a minibar. Back home the hotel wouldn't get a single star, but still. At a short briefing on the garden terrace of the hotel restaurant we met our two fellow trekkers, Harald from Austria and Alexander, and our mountain guide Ram (photo, left to right: Chris, Alex, Harry, Ralf).

We drank a few tasty Tuborgs and had a bite to eat – very cheap and perfectly edible, by the way – and then went up to the room, as we were due at breakfast at 5 the next morning.

Back in the room – somewhere in Kathmandu a bar was blasting rock music in the style of TNT – we had our first encounter with our own chaos.

Because now we had to decide what to take in the daypack and what goes in the big duffel bag carried by the Sherpas. I chose camera and wide-angle lens, passport and money, a few important medicines, rain trousers and jacket, sweatshirt, energy bars, water bladder, hat and wet wipes. That made the daypack pretty heavy already. The rest went into the duffel bag, which you only see again in the evening at the destination.

It all took quite a while, though, because on the one hand my wound disinfectant spray had leaked and I had to dry everything. On the other hand I had to pack and unpack several times because I'd forgotten something.

Finally Ralf and I were done and called our loved ones once more. Unfortunately Ralf had no reception and nobody could call me. So we only spoke briefly with Mallorca and Istanbul.

Dead tired, I fell asleep shortly afterwards – at about 1 am. Ralf probably didn't get to sleep until later.

Day 1 was over.
