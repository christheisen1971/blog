DEENTR

Day 14 · October 1, 2009

Kala Patthar (5,550 m) and Pheriche (4,280 m)

Translated from German

Today the second 5,000-metre peak was on the list: Kala Patthar.

From the lodge in Gorak Shep we climbed Kala Patthar at 6:30. After about 100 minutes we had covered the 420 metres of ascent to the summit at 5,550 m. The highest point I have ever reached on foot. But every step was hard. The air made breathing so laborious that I had to pause every few steps. It was especially tough because the path is strewn with stones and boulders. When a stretch came that was free of stones, you could find a slow pace and go up step by step. But as soon as there was a rock to climb over, I lost my rhythm and my breathing faltered.

At the top we had a fantastic view of many mountains. Including Mount Everest (see photo, middle peak), which had been "asleep" the day before, as the Nepalis say of a mountain covered by a cloud.

Everest – whose real name (like the national park) is Sagarmatha – doesn't look that special and isn't even that easy to see. Other mountains are much more beautiful. We took lots of photos and enjoyed the moments. I also went right up to the very top, but I felt pretty uneasy, as it dropped away very steeply. Those colourful prayer flags hang everywhere. They always have 5 colours: blue stands for the sky, white for the clouds, red for fire, green for water and yellow for earth.

The descent from Kala Patthar went comparatively quickly. After that there was finally breakfast, and we set off for Pheriche.

The way there went mainly downhill – counting from the top of Kala Patthar, more than 1,200 metres. On the way we noticed the change in nature: there's much more green and many more animals. Along the way we passed a memorial for those who died on Mount Everest. Very moving.

Our destination, Pheriche, is a place that can actually be called a village, whereas the other places are just clusters of lodges for tourists. We found a great lodge here. Next door there's an internet café and even a German bakery, which we haven't tried yet.

Tomorrow we continue downhill to Tengboche, where there's an important monastery.
