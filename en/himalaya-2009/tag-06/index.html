DEENTR

Day 6 · September 23, 2009

Dole – 4,080 m

Translated from German

Today we completed a double stage and are now in Dole at 4,080 m.

The reason was that we want to walk with Stefan and Sandra as long as possible, but they're only doing a tour up to the summit of Gokyo Ri. So their tour is shorter, and the alternative would have been for them to walk more slowly with us and skip an acclimatisation day. But that's not advisable at these altitudes, so we decided to do a double stage today. Otherwise we would have stayed in Khumjung.

Since we also had to go downhill in between, the vegetation changed several times today. After a steep climb we soon reached altitudes where there are hardly any trees left. There are steep meadows with lots of colourful high-alpine flowers, a bit like gentian or edelweiss. Yaks and naks – as the female yaks are called – graze there. These burly animals came towards us often today – sometimes in very narrow spots. They're either being driven to pasture or used as pack animals. On the descent we walked through woodland again that looks almost like Central Europe.

The paths are really very hard going, uphill or downhill. Clay paths are the easiest. But the stony paths, partly covered with loose rubble or big boulders, are very tiring to hike. You can only go very slowly. In total we were on the move for about 8 hours with a break of about 2 hours.

A little anecdote at this point. There are lots of big, fat birds here – a kind of raven that makes itself heard quite loudly. There's also a kind of blackbird, but smaller. When we sat down for lunch (see photo: Sandra, Stefan, Alexander), I put my "dessert" – my energy bar – on a little wall next to me. During the meal one of the ravens suddenly swooped down on this treat – and it was gone. At first none of us really noticed. But then it was clear, and someone even ran after the bird. But it was long gone. Well, and then I also got to listen to the ravens' croaking, which sounded just like laughter. Thanks a lot.

Today's destination – the village of Dole – isn't really a village. There are 3–4 lodges here. Nothing more. Each guesthouse has several rooms, cells of about 2.5 m x 2.5 m partitioned off with plywood, each with 2 cots. Toilet and squat toilet are in the corridor. The shower is outside. Although "shower" is an overstatement. You get a tub of hot water and can wash with it. Ralf, Stefan and Harald did without and washed in the cold stream. I preferred to skip it, because the water was truly ice-cold.

Every guesthouse has a heated dining room. All the guests meet and eat there. The menus are the same everywhere. Rice, noodles, potatoes. Mostly with vegetables and/or eggs. There's little meat, and you shouldn't eat it anyway. The soups are good and tasty. An important one is garlic soup, as garlic is supposed to help prevent altitude sickness.

Today I had a headache for the first time and took an Aspirin 500. Then it got better. It was probably the wind today. You sweat a lot, and it's windy and shady. At this altitude there are always clouds at the moment, so you can't really see the mountains above us. You also have to watch the path like a hawk, because on one side it usually drops away steeply. And there are stones and yak dung everywhere. Today we watched the animals descend those "steps" that we can barely manage. Unbelievable.

Our group now consists of 6 participants plus 2 guides and 3 Sherpas. What those guys carry is hard to believe. And on paths like these. Steeply uphill and steeply downhill. I have the greatest respect for them. I've learned a little Nepali:

Right, I'll call it a day. It's only 8:20 pm, but the day was exhausting. And we had no phone network all day today. So there's not much I can do or anyone I can call anyway. Good night.
